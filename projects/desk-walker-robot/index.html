Walking Robot

ROS2 and Gazebo simulation of a two-legged Robot

Back to Portfolio

This project consists on a simulation of a walking two-legged robot, with a frontal camera. The simulation allows to integrate multiple tools: the 3D model building, the Gazebo configuration with moving parts, and the ROS2 communication of the different modules.

The robot is conformed by a main body which holds a camera in the middle front, two vertical legs (Y-axis, normal to the floor) that have one degree of freedom to rotate around their geometrical axis; and two plates that work as feet, and have one degrees of freedom that is in the Z-axis, that allows the robot to tilt from side to side.

The tools and skills used for the simulation are the following:

  • Xacro is used to generate the 3D model upon the URDF format, defining moving parts, collision boxes and inertia of each part.
  • RViz is used to visually verify the 3D model, and the correct communication between ROS2 and Gazebo, by validating collisions, movements and the camera image.
  • Python is used to create nodes, services, topics and parameters in ROS2, allowing to move the robot within the simulation through ROS2 commands.
  • ROS2 is used to integrate the whole system, setting the 3D model in the simulation space, enabling the camera, and allowing communication and control of the system.
  • Gazebo is used as the simulation and physics engine, interpreting the model in a physical space, and allowing to capture image through the camera and sending it back to ROS2 along with all the joints and movement data.

The walking sequence of the robot goes as follows: Starting from a position with both feet on the floor. The robot tilts to one side, putting all the weight in foot 1 so the foot 2 is in the air. This movement uses foot 1 and foot 2 Z-axis rotation to displace the center of mass. Next, the robot rotates over foot 1 so foot 2 moves in the forward direction, still in the air. This movement uses hip rotation from leg 1 Y-axis. From here, the robot tilts back to putting both feet in the floor, but with foot 2 ahead of foot 1 in the walking direction. Next step is to repeat the process tilting the robot over foot 2, to perform the second step forward of the robot. The process causes a waddle walking.

Some stages are still in progress for this projects. An AutoDesk Fusion360 model is in progress for a manufacturable version of the robot, for which the electronics components are being selected. And the walking sequence is yet to be programmed, since currently the robot simulation can only move using one-by-one ROS2 commands for each step.
